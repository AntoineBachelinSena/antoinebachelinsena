01 — THE JURISDICTIONAL GAP

Strategic risk does not stop at administrative borders.

The same activity can look very different from one jurisdiction to another. A gold export, a mining title, a company or a financial transaction may be recorded differently depending on the system in which it appears.

That is where part of the risk lies.

I compare the records, follow discrepancies across jurisdictions, and establish what can — and cannot — be demonstrated.

03 — METHOD

The starting point is the source material.

I work from primary sources wherever possible: official registries, cadastral records, trade statistics, legal documents and institutional records.

Each assessment distinguishes between established facts, inferences, working hypotheses and information that cannot be established from the available material.

Data gaps and conflicting records are documented rather than hidden.

Primary sources Official registries, cadastral records, trade statistics, legal documents and institutional records wherever available.
Source status Clear separation between established facts, inferences, working hypotheses and information that cannot be established.
Cross-jurisdiction comparison Records are compared across France, Brazil, Suriname and Guyana rather than read in isolation.
Limits stated Missing data, conflicting records and unresolved ambiguities are identified explicitly.

07 — ABOUT

Antoine Bachelin Sena

Antoine Bachelin Sena is a French structural risk analyst based in São Paulo, Brazil, since 2011.

His work focuses on cross-border risk across the Guiana Shield and the South Atlantic, with particular attention to illicit gold flows, mining rights, critical minerals and maritime jurisdiction.

He works across French, Portuguese, Spanish and English sources, comparing legal, administrative and statistical records across France, Brazil, Suriname and Guyana.

He is Director of Strategy at the Institut Atlantique Sud.
